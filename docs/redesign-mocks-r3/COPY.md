# Round-3 copy deck

The text source for every round-3 mock. It is deliberately short: round 2 had about 3,500 words of copy on the page, and this deck aims for 600–900 per mock.

**Sources:**

- [`../branding-website-content.md`](../branding-website-content.md): the content plan. Its §11 holds the claims rules.
- [`../redesign-mocks-r2/COPY.md`](../redesign-mocks-r2/COPY.md): the vetted industry figures and their sources.

**Rules:**

- **Claims cover only what is built**, per content plan §11. AOS (operations) is "later", never a feature. No savings figures, no customer logos, no certifications.
- **Put at most two sourced industry figures on a page.** Each one links to a single short Sources note in the footer. No sidenotes, no dagger forest.
- **Never use these words:** "revolutionary", "disrupt", "game-changing", "AI-powered", "unlock", "seamless", "leverage", "empower", "next-gen".
- **Never put "Palantir" on the page.**
- **Mocks may cut, reorder and choose among options. They may not invent claims.**

---

## Two voices (each mock declares one)

**Bold:** ambition first, stated plainly, then a concrete fact within the same screen. Short, declarative sentences.

**Engineer:** precise and understated. It reads the way one engineer writes to another. The ambition shows through specifics, not adjectives.

## 2 · The problem (three short points)

**Headline options:**

- Bold: *Megaprojects don't fail in the plan. They fail between the plan and the site.*
- Engineer: *Four systems, one project, and nothing connecting them.*

**The three points:**

- **Tenders take weeks to read.** Hundreds of documents, addenda arriving daily, and requirements buried in specs.
- **Bids and builds don't talk.** The estimate lives in one file, the schedule in another, the site reports in a third.
- **Overruns show up too late.** By the time a deviation reaches the monthly report, the money is already spent.

**One figure (optional, pick one):**

- **96%:** the average cost overrun across 245 large dams in 65 countries.¹
- **98%:** the share of megaprojects that overrun their budget by more than 30%.²

## 3 · The lifecycle (the main section)

**Headline options:**

- Bold: *One system, from the first page of the tender to the last pour.*
- Engineer: *How DiPGOS carries a project through.*

| # | Step | One line (the site copy) | Module |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Read the tender** | Upload the package. DiPGOS extracts the synopsis, client, value, dates and bid bond, and runs your bid / no-bid decision with the right approvals. | CPDS |
| 2 | **Understand the requirements** | Requirements are extracted for six disciplines (civil, electrical, mechanical, piping, process, I&C) and mapped to the project's areas. Engineers approve them; nothing goes forward unchecked. | CPDS |
| 3 | **Build the proposal** | Requirements become deliverables, deliverables become operations, and operations get their crews, equipment and materials. Schedule and cost derive themselves. | CPDS |
| 4 | **Export the bid** | The budget sheet comes out in Excel and the proposal in Word, with the proposal manager's commercial buffers applied. | CPDS |
| 5 | **Book real resources** | Once the work is won, planned resource types become real, booked people, machines and materials, with work fronts, critical path and baselines. | Atom Manager |
| 6 | **Control construction** | Every project, from the portfolio down to a single operation, on a live map. Site teams report; alarms flag deviations; changes and cost increases show as they happen. | ACCS |
| — | **Operate** *(later)* | The asset's life after handover. Planned, not built. | AOS |

**The loop variant (for mocks that keep R1-C's ring):** the six steps form a cycle, with what construction learns feeding the next tender. **The return arrow's label:** "What you built prices what you bid next." *This is a design idea, not a product claim. Do not say the product does it automatically.*
